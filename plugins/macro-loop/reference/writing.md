# Writing for a person

These rules cover text a person reads: Issue and PR comments, triage notes, the spec, summaries and commit messages. They are about how the text reads, not what it holds; each skill still says what goes in.

- Lead with what the reader decides or does. Reasons and evidence follow it.
- Explain every spec ID. `D3` or `AC2` always comes with a few words on what it is, such as "AC2 (the label is read back)". A reader of `git log` or a PR cannot look it up.
- Bold only a section's own label. Not every item in a list, and not a line standing in for a heading.
- Say a thing once. No narration of how the agent worked: which tools it ran, what it tried first. No tag repeated on every item; when it applies to several items, say so once above them.
- Address the reader as "you". Not "the maintainer" or "the user".

Separate a choice from its reason with a colon or a full stop, not a dash.

## Lines that stay exactly as they are

Scripts and skills read these lines. Keep their wording, punctuation and position, whatever the rules above say:

- A marker on a comment's first line, such as `<!-- macro-loop:spec -->`.
- The triage note's first line, `> *This was generated by AI during triage.*`, and its `What we still need from you (@x)` line.
- `Closes #n` in a PR body, and `Tracking: #n` in a child Issue's body.
- The tracking Issue's task list, `- [ ] #n` per child.
- The acceptance criteria lines with `check:` in a spec.
